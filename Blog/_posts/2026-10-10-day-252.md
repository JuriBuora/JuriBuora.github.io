# 🔄 Topic

I reached a useful milestone in my Google Cybersecurity Certificate: completion certificates for Foundations of Cybersecurity, Play It Safe: Manage Security Risks, Assets, Threats, and Vulnerabilities, Tools of the Trade: Linux and SQL, and Connect and Protect: Networks and Network Security.

The certificates are evidence that I completed the courses. They are not a substitute for work experience, and they do not mean that I can stop practising. The more valuable result is the way the subjects now connect.

# 🎯 Goal

Turn a sequence of course modules into a coherent security workflow rather than treating each topic as an isolated vocabulary list.

# 🛠 What I Built Into My Mental Model

The foundation course gave me the purpose and language of the field. Risk management gave me a way to think about likelihood, impact, controls, and priorities. Asset and vulnerability lessons made the protected thing explicit before discussing threats.

Linux and SQL made the work more concrete. I can use the command line to inspect systems and SQL to retrieve, filter, sort, and combine security-relevant records. Networking added the path between systems: protocols, packet headers, addresses, ports, and tools such as Wireshark.

The current incident-response material added the operational loop. Detection creates evidence, analysts investigate and escalate, technical leads coordinate containment and recovery, and documentation preserves what was learned.

# 🧠 What I Learned

Cybersecurity is not one tool or one job title. It is a chain:

1. identify assets and the business context;
2. understand threats, vulnerabilities, and likely impact;
3. collect and filter useful evidence;
4. detect and investigate unusual activity;
5. coordinate response and recovery;
6. document the outcome and improve the controls.

The courses also changed how I describe my own work. I should say exactly what I studied, what I practised, what was simulated, and what remains unverified. Honest boundaries make a learning portfolio more credible, not less.

# ⚠️ Limitations

These certificates document course completion. They do not claim professional employment, production incident response, or independent enterprise experience. My labs and posts remain training exercises unless I explicitly identify a separate real implementation and its evidence.

# ✅ Takeaway

Finishing the courses is a milestone, not the finish line. I now have a connected foundation for practising packet analysis, SQL investigation, Linux operations, risk assessment, and incident response with better questions and more disciplined evidence.
